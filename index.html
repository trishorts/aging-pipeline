Aging proteomics repository

Public proteomics data, reanalysed with one pipeline, for questions about aging.

Do organelles age at different rates?

This is the data repository of an NCEMS working group on aging. The group asks whether organelles age at different rates, and whether proteoform- and PTM-level measurements can see aging signal that bulk protein abundance misses.

To answer that, it reanalyses public aging proteomics data from PRIDE with one pipeline, so that datasets from different labs, instruments and years can be compared. Every dataset is searched from its raw spectra, never from the authors' processed tables, and every number keeps a record of the exact files, tools and settings that made it.

How it works

  • aging-pipeline finds aging datasets in PRIDE, downloads and quality-checks the raw spectra, and searches and quantifies them with MetaMorpheus, including GPTMD to discover post-translational modifications, and FlashLFQ.
  • dataRepo stores the results in one schema and serves them to people and to AI agents. This site is generated from its catalog.

Where it stands. The repository holds identifications, quantities and PTM sites. It does not yet hold age effects, organelle assignments or sample ages, so it cannot answer the group's question on its own yet. Those are the next stages of the pipeline. Each dataset page lists the known problems with that dataset. Please read them before using its numbers.

Many datasets are enrichments, not whole proteomes. Immunoprecipitations, proximity-labelling and affinity pulldowns, and chemical-probe captures are kept, because an organelle pulldown is an organelle proteome, and each is labelled with its enrichment. Their intensities describe the captured material. Do not read them as whole-cell abundance, or pool them with whole proteomes.

Built at the Smith lab, University of Wisconsin–Madison. Questions and problems: please open an issue on aging-pipeline.
