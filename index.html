Aging proteomics repository

Public proteomics data, reanalysed with one pipeline, for questions about how organelle proteomes change with age.

Do organelles age at different rates?

This is the data repository of an NCEMS working group on aging. The group asks whether organelles age at different rates, and whether proteoform- and PTM-level measurements can see aging signal that bulk protein abundance misses.

To answer that, it reanalyses public aging proteomics data from PRIDE with one pipeline, so that datasets from different labs, instruments and years can be compared. Every dataset is searched from its raw spectra, never from the authors' processed tables, and every number keeps a record of the exact files, tools and settings that made it.

How it works

  • aging-pipeline finds aging datasets in PRIDE, downloads and quality-checks the raw spectra, and searches and quantifies them with MetaMorpheus, including GPTMD to discover post-translational modifications, and FlashLFQ.
  • dataRepo stores the results in one schema and serves them to people and to AI agents. This site is generated from its catalog.

Where it stands

  • 92 datasets, 48 of them whole proteomes (no enrichment step): human 40 (24 whole); mouse 38 (22 whole); rat 14 (2 whole).
  • Enrichments: affinity_purification 15; immunoprecipitation 7; proximity_labelling 7; phospho 6; other 4; chemical_probe 3; mixed runs 2.
  • Beyond identifications and quantities: per-run PTM site occupancy (2,182,220 rows, 92 datasets) and protein-to-gene mappings (1,099,099 rows). These, and every individual protein, are in the catalog and reachable through the MCP server, not on these pages. The catalog file itself is not published yet.
  • Sample ages: 320 runs in PXD031782, PXD047289, PXD047292, PXD047293, PXD051203, PXD051644, PXD056433, PXD056458, PXD058248, PXD058253, curated from the depositors' own records and paper.
  • Not held yet: age effects, organelle assignments, and an experimental design for any dataset. Every dataset was quantified with each raw file as its own condition.
  • These counts are from catalog f947dc8cb18c2ba2.

So the repository cannot answer the group's question on its own yet: ages, designs and organelle assignments are the next stages of the pipeline. Each dataset page lists the known problems with that dataset. Please read them before using its numbers.

Use it with your own AI agent

Any assistant that can read a web page (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, or your own agent with a fetch tool) can work from this site. There is nothing to install.

  • Start here. Begin your conversation with: "Read https://trishorts.github.io/aging-pipeline/llms.txt and follow its rules. Then answer: ...". llms.txt lists every dataset with a one-sentence summary, and the rules that keep answers honest.
  • For one dataset's details, use its page: https://trishorts.github.io/aging-pipeline/datasets/<accession>.html (for example datasets/PXD036557.html). Each page gives the dataset's counts, quality metrics, most frequent modifications and every known problem.
  • Prefer the pages to datasets.json. It holds the same facts for every dataset in one file, but it is large enough that many fetch tools cut it off without saying so, and an agent can then miss datasets without knowing.
  • Ask it to cite. Every number here comes from one catalog build. Have your agent quote the catalog id shown above with any number it reuses.

Questions that work from these pages today:

  • "Which datasets in this repository are whole-proteome mouse samples?"
  • "Which dataset has the most PSMs at 1% FDR, and how many?"
  • "Why does PXD051644 have fewer runs than PRIDE lists?"
  • "What are the open warnings for PXD036557, and what do they mean for using its numbers?"
  • "Which datasets have sample ages?"

Protein-level questions need more than the website (next section). Until then, "not available from the site" is the correct answer to a protein-level question, and a good agent will say so.

How you will be able to query the full catalog (not available yet)

None of this works yet. The catalog is a single database file (DuckDB) too large for this website, and it is not yet on a public server. When it is, the link and install instructions will appear here, and the steps will be:

  • Download the catalog file. It is one file, named after the catalog id shown above.
  • Install the repository's query tool (instructions will come with the download; note that an unrelated package with the same name exists on PyPI).
  • Connect your agent. For Claude Code: datarepo mcp --catalog <file> --install, then restart Claude Code. For any other assistant that supports MCP servers, add datarepo mcp --catalog <file> as a local (stdio) server. datarepo mcp --catalog <file> --check confirms the file opens.
  • Or query it directly with DuckDB and SQL. The *_1pct views (psms_1pct, peptidoforms_1pct, protein_groups_1pct) already apply the 1% FDR rule.

Your agent then gets three tools: describe (what the catalog holds, and what every table and column means), search (look up a dataset, protein, gene, peptide or modification) and sql (read-only queries). The biology it is built for:

  • Does aging change how proteins are tagged, not just how much? "Across the four rat hippocampus and cortex lifespan datasets (3, 12 and 24 months), which proteins gain degradation tags (K48 polyubiquitin) with age, and are they the same ones that lose signalling tags (M1 and K63)?"
  • Aging that abundance clocks cannot see. "In the rat lifespan datasets, which modification sites change in how often they are occupied between young and old animals while the protein's own amount stays flat?"
  • Molecular timers. "Within the lens and cartilage datasets, do long-lived proteins such as crystallins and collagens carry more asparagine deamidation, a spontaneous chemical clock, than short-lived proteins from the same tissue?"
  • The senescent cell. "In senescent versus young human mammary epithelial cells (PXD027318; the run names mark the groups), do lysosomal proteins rise and does the nuclear lamina protein lamin B1 fall?"
  • Organelles in the aging human heart (the dataset is being added): "In the human left ventricle, do mitochondrial proteins decline with age faster than the rest of the proteome, in failing and non-failing hearts alike?"

Every answer is exploratory. The datasets are small, and the sample ages are curated by hand from each deposit's own paper and record. Most of the rat datasets are ubiquitin pulldowns rather than whole tissue, so a change there means a change in what was tagged, not in how much of the protein exists.

Before you use these numbers

  • A dataset's organism is the species of the protein database it was searched against. It is not an independent check of the sample, and no cell line or tissue is recorded yet. Where a title disagrees (PXD050351 says "in mice"; its samples are a human cell line), the searched species is the one to trust.
  • A deposited SDRF labels the samples; it is not an experimental design. Where one exists it names organism, tissue and replicate, and no dataset has conditions assigned to runs yet.
  • Protein-group counts at 1% FDR include contaminant groups. The MCP server's views say so and let you separate them.
  • In mouse and rat datasets, some groups labelled contaminant are native proteins. The search's contaminant panel carries some ordinary human proteins (for example tau, creatine kinase M and peroxiredoxin-1), and rodent peptides shared with them are credited to the panel. Contamination figures for those datasets are therefore somewhat overstated.
  • One chemistry can appear under two modification names (phosphoserine is listed both under its UniProt name and its search-engine name). Count by chemistry, not by name.

Many datasets are enrichments, not whole proteomes. Immunoprecipitations, proximity-labelling and affinity pulldowns, and chemical-probe captures are kept, because an organelle pulldown is an organelle proteome, and each is labelled with its enrichment. Their intensities describe the captured material. Do not read them as whole-cell abundance, or pool them with whole proteomes.
